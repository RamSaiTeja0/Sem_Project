## 1. BASIC PROJECT QUESTIONS

### Q1.1: What is TecSubstitution?
> **Answer**: TecSubstitution is a centralized academic timetable and faculty substitution management platform designed for polytechnic and engineering colleges. It digitally manages class schedules, tracks daily teacher absences, evaluates teacher availability in real time, and allows teachers to arrange peer substitutions without double-booking.

### Q1.2: Why did you choose this project?
> **Answer**: In our college, morning faculty absences create sudden vacant classes. Finding a substitute teacher manually through phone calls or staff room searches is chaotic, takes too long, and often causes double-booking. We chose this project to replace those slow, manual methods with an automated, conflict-free digital system.

### Q1.3: What problem does it solve?
> **Answer**: It solves the problems of lost instructional hours, teacher double-booking, room clashes, uncoordinated exam invigilation duties, and the lack of departmental record-keeping during faculty absences.

### Q1.4: What is the main objective?
> **Answer**: The main objective is to provide a conflict-free, branch-isolated platform where an absent teacher can locate an available colleague and complete a substitution in minutes, while keeping master timetables safe and intact.

### Q1.5: Who are the users of the system?
> **Answer**: The system serves two primary authenticated users:
> 1. **Head of Section (HOS)**: The departmental administrator who manages the branch, faculty roster, class timetable, daily attendance, and exam duties.
> 2. **Faculty Members**: The teaching staff who view their personal timetables, check colleague availability, and send/accept peer substitution requests.

### Q1.6: What is the main feature of TecSubstitution?
> **Answer**: The main feature is the **Peer-to-Peer Faculty Substitution Workflow (Option A)**, where an absent teacher selects a free colleague, dispatches a substitution request, and the colleague accepts it with real-time atomic availability revalidation.

### Q1.7: What is the main advantage over a manual system?
> **Answer**: It completely eliminates double-booking through application checks and PostgreSQL database constraints, and reduces substitution arrangement time from 30 minutes of manual inquiry down to under two minutes.

### Q1.8: What is the scope of the project?
> **Answer**: The scope covers multi-tenant branch isolation, master and faculty timetables, daily absence logging, exam invigilation scheduling, availability calculation, peer substitution, and AI-assisted timetable extraction with HOS approval.

### Q1.9: What are the current limitations?
> **Answer**: It requires an active internet connection for cloud PostgreSQL and Gemini AI, handles 1-to-1 period substitutions rather than splitting a period across multiple teachers, and uses signed session cookies rather than college-wide Single Sign-On (SSO).

### Q1.10: What is the future scope?
> **Answer**: Future scope includes automated SMS/WhatsApp alerts, smart AI recommendation scoring based on teacher workloads, visual analytics dashboards, automated timetable generation from scratch, and native mobile apps.

## 2. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE QUESTIONS

### Q2.1: Explain the system architecture.
> **Answer**: TecSubstitution follows a multi-tier client-server architecture:
> - **Client Tier**: Responsive HTML5, Vanilla CSS, and JavaScript running in user browsers.
> - **Application Server**: Node.js and Express.js providing REST APIs, signed cookie sessions, and business logic.
> - **Database Tier**: Remote serverless PostgreSQL hosted on Neon with connection pooling and SSL encryption.
> - **AI Pipeline**: Decoupled n8n workflow and Google Gemini Vision for document ingestion.

### Q2.2: Why did you use Frontend + Node.js/Express + PostgreSQL?
> **Answer**: 
> - **Frontend**: Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS delivers fast load times and clean user interaction without heavy framework dependencies.
> - **Node.js / Express**: Event-driven, non-blocking asynchronous architecture easily handles simultaneous teacher requests.
> - **PostgreSQL**: Timetables have strict relational foreign key dependencies, and PostgreSQL enforces unique constraints to physically prevent double-booking.

### Q2.3: What does the backend do?
> **Answer**: The backend handles user authentication, cryptographic cookie signing, role authorization, department scoping, slot conflict checks, real-time availability calculations, and PostgreSQL database queries.

### Q2.4: What does the frontend do?
> **Answer**: The frontend captures user input, displays interactive weekly timetables and laboratory spans, renders candidate cards, manages incoming notification badges, and communicates asynchronously with backend REST endpoints using the `fetch` API.

### Q2.5: How does data flow through the system?
> **Answer**: User requests send signed session cookies to Express routes. Route middleware validates the user's role and branch. Business logic queries PostgreSQL using parameterized SQL, evaluates availability or conflict rules, and returns JSON to the browser to dynamically update the DOM.

### Q2.6: Why is the database separated from the AI pipeline?
> **Answer**: To protect academic data integrity. Gemini AI and n8n operate in an isolated staging workflow and hold **zero credentials** to the live database. This ensures AI output cannot silently corrupt or overwrite production schedules without human HOS approval.

## 3. TECHNOLOGY QUESTIONS

### Q3.1: Why Vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript?
> **Answer**: Vanilla technologies provide maximum flexibility, zero build-step overhead, fast browser rendering, and full control over styling custom properties and DOM elements without framework bloat.

### Q3.2: Why Node.js?
> **Answer**: Node.js uses an event-driven, non-blocking I/O model that is lightweight and highly efficient for data-intensive real-time applications where multiple teachers check schedules simultaneously.

### Q3.3: Why Express.js?
> **Answer**: Express is a minimalist, flexible web framework that simplifies route creation, middleware integration, error handling, and JSON API design.

### Q3.4: Why PostgreSQL?
> **Answer**: Academic scheduling is deeply relational. PostgreSQL provides strict referential integrity, ACID transactions, and partial unique indexes to guarantee that neither teachers nor classrooms can be double-booked.

### Q3.5: Why Neon?
> **Answer**: Neon provides serverless cloud-hosted PostgreSQL with SSL encryption and connection pooling. It makes our database accessible over the internet without running a local database server on our computer.

### Q3.6: Why n8n?
> **Answer**: n8n is an open-source visual workflow engine. It handles webhook dispatches, communicates with the Gemini API, and processes file binaries asynchronously without blocking our main web server.

### Q3.7: Why Google Gemini?
> **Answer**: Google Gemini 2.5 Flash has multimodal vision capabilities, allowing it to accurately read complex visual grid structures, periods, and subject abbreviations from scanned JPEG and PDF timetable sheets.

### Q3.8: Why REST APIs?
> **Answer**: REST APIs use standard HTTP methods (`GET`, `POST`, `PUT`, `DELETE`) with JSON payloads, making client-server communication clean, modular, and easy to test.

### Q3.9: What are webhooks and why are they used?
> **Answer**: A webhook is an automated HTTP POST callback triggered by an event. When an HOS uploads a timetable file, our backend fires a webhook to n8n to start the extraction workflow in the background.

### Q3.10: Why Git and GitHub?
> **Answer**: Git provides distributed version control to track code changes and resolve conflicts, while GitHub hosts the remote repository for collaboration and project synchronization.

## 4. DATABASE QUESTIONS

### Q4.1: Why a relational database instead of NoSQL?
> **Answer**: Academic scheduling is inherently relational. Departments have faculty, faculty teach subjects, subjects belong to classes, and classes occupy rooms. Relational databases enforce foreign key relationships and unique indexes, which NoSQL databases cannot guarantee easily.

### Q4.2: What are the main tables in your schema?
> **Answer**: The main tables are:
> - `departments`, `rooms`, `subjects`, `classes`, `periods` (Catalog)
> - `faculty`, `faculty_subjects`, `users` (Users & Roster)
> - `timetable` (Live Master Schedule)
> - `faculty_attendance` (Daily Absence Records)
> - `exam_invigilation` & `exam_invigilation_requests` (Exam Duties)
> - `faculty_substitutions` (Peer Substitutions Overlay)
> - `timetable_uploads` & `timetable_staging` (AI Ingestion Pipeline)

### Q4.3: How are faculty and timetable related?
> **Answer**: The `timetable` table has a foreign key `faculty_id REFERENCES faculty(id)`. A partial unique index `(faculty_id, day_of_week, period)` ensures an instructor cannot be linked to two classes during the same period.

### Q4.4: How are classes and subjects related?
> **Answer**: Both have a foreign key pointing to `departments(id)`. When an entry is placed in `timetable`, it references both `class_id` and `subject_id`, ensuring subjects are taught to designated class sections.

### Q4.5: How is branch information stored?
> **Answer**: Stored in the `departments` table with columns `code` (e.g., `CME`), `name`, `academic_year`, and `semester`. All related entities reference `department_id` or store `department_code`.

### Q4.6: How is data persistence achieved?
> **Answer**: All data is written to PostgreSQL disk storage on Neon. Even if the Node.js application server stops, crashes, or restarts, all records are reloaded cleanly upon reboot via `store.initFromDatabase()`.

### Q4.7: How do database constraints help?
> **Answer**: Database constraints act as an unbreakable safety net. Even if a bug existed in client code, constraints like `timetable_class_slot_unique` and `exam_invigilation_unique` reject duplicate entries at the database engine level.

### Q4.8: How do you prevent duplicate or conflicting records?
> **Answer**: Using unique constraints:
> - `UNIQUE (class_id, day_of_week, period)` prevents class slot collisions.
> - Partial unique index on `(faculty_id, day_of_week, period)` prevents faculty double-booking.
> - Partial unique index on `(room_id, day_of_week, period)` prevents room clashes.

### Q4.9: What happens when the application restarts?
> **Answer**: During startup (`bootstrap()`), the server connects to Neon PostgreSQL, runs idempotent schema migrations, loads active data into cache, and starts listening. Zero data is lost.

## 5. AUTHENTICATION AND SECURITY

### Q5.1: How does login work?
> **Answer**: The user submits their username and password to `POST /api/auth/login`. The server verifies credentials against hashed passwords in the `users` table and returns a cryptographically signed session cookie (`tec_session`).

### Q5.2: How are sessions handled?
> **Answer**: We use stateless signed cookies. The session payload (`id`, `username`, `role`, `department`) is Base64URL-encoded and signed with an HMAC-SHA256 hash using a secret server key. The server validates the signature on every request.

### Q5.3: What is role-based authorization?
> **Answer**: Restricting actions based on user role (`hos` vs `faculty`). Express middleware (`requireHOS`, `requireFaculty`) intercepts unauthorized requests with HTTP 403 Forbidden.

### Q5.4: What can the HOS do?
> **Answer**: The HOS can manage branch settings, faculty accounts, classes, subjects, master timetable entries, daily faculty attendance, exam invigilation assignments, and AI staging approvals. HOS has read-only access to substitutions.

### Q5.5: What can Faculty do?
> **Answer**: Faculty can view their personal timetable, check colleague availability, send substitution requests, accept/reject incoming substitution requests, view personal attendance history, and request exam duties.

### Q5.6: How is branch isolation enforced?
> **Answer**: The user's department is locked into the session token. Every database query filters by `department_code = req.session.department`. Cross-branch mutations are rejected with HTTP 403 Forbidden.

### Q5.7: Can Faculty access another branch's data?
> **Answer**: No. Faculty can only view free teacher names from other branches during an availability search; they cannot access or modify other branches' administrative records.

### Q5.8: Can Faculty change their branch?
> **Answer**: No. When an HOS creates a faculty member, the branch code is permanently inherited. Faculty profiles have no option to change departments.

### Q5.9: What happens when a faculty account is deactivated?
> **Answer**: The account's `active` column in the `users` table is set to `FALSE`. Login attempts are rejected, preventing access while preserving historical audit records.

### Q5.10: How are passwords protected?
> **Answer**: Passwords are encrypted using cryptographic hashing before being stored in the database. Plaintext passwords are never stored, logged, or returned in API responses.

### Q5.11: How are database secrets protected?
> **Answer**: Database credentials and API keys are stored strictly in server-side environment variables (`.env`) and are never exposed to the frontend or committed to Git.

## 6. TIMETABLE QUESTIONS

### Q6.1: How is the timetable stored?
> **Answer**: In the `timetable` table, where each row represents an atomic class period linked to `class_id`, `day_of_week`, `period`, `subject_id`, `faculty_id`, and `room_id`.

### Q6.2: What is the master timetable?
> **Answer**: The master timetable is the institutional schedule displaying all classes, sections, subjects, and rooms across all six days of the week for an entire department.

### Q6.3: What is a faculty timetable?
> **Answer**: A personalized view that filters the master timetable to display only the classes assigned to a specific instructor.

### Q6.4: How are semester, year, and section scopes handled?
> **Answer**: The `classes` table defines class sections (e.g., `CME-A`), linking them to an academic year (e.g., `2025-2026`), semester level (1 to 6), and section letter (`A`, `B`).

### Q6.5: How are laboratory practicals represented?
> **Answer**: Laboratory practicals spanning multiple periods (e.g., Periods 1 to 3) are expanded into atomic period rows in the database, while the frontend visually merges them across columns with a distinct badge.

### Q6.6: How are non-teaching activities represented?
> **Answer**: Activities like Library, TPC, and Sports have `session_type = 'activity'` and a `NULL` faculty ID. They occupy the class slot without requiring a teacher assignment.

### Q6.7: How do you prevent timetable conflicts?
> **Answer**: Through in-memory validation before inserting and PostgreSQL unique constraints that physically reject colliding records.

### Q6.8: How do you prevent faculty double-booking?
> **Answer**: The partial unique index `timetable_faculty_slot_unique` on `(faculty_id, day_of_week, period)` ensures an instructor cannot be scheduled for two classes in the same period.

### Q6.9: How do you prevent room conflicts?
> **Answer**: The partial unique index `timetable_room_slot_unique` on `(room_id, day_of_week, period)` ensures a classroom or lab cannot host two different classes simultaneously.

### Q6.10: Does an accepted substitution modify the master timetable?
> **Answer**: **No.** The master timetable is 100% untouched. Accepted substitutions are recorded as separate overlay records in `faculty_substitutions`.

## 7. ATTENDANCE QUESTIONS

### Q7.1: What does ABSENT mean?
> **Answer**: It means the faculty member is on leave or not available on campus for that entire calendar date.

### Q7.2: Is attendance date-specific?
> **Answer**: Yes. Attendance is recorded against a specific calendar date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) in `faculty_attendance` and does not affect other dates or recurring weekly timetables.

### Q7.3: Who can mark absence?
> **Answer**: Only the Head of Section (HOS) or administrative coordinator can mark or edit attendance.

### Q7.4: Can Faculty modify attendance?
> **Answer**: No. Faculty members have strictly read-only access to view their personal attendance history.

### Q7.5: What happens to an absent faculty member in the availability engine?
> **Answer**: They are automatically marked BUSY and excluded from being suggested as a substitute candidate for any period on that date.

### Q7.6: What happens when the faculty member becomes PRESENT again?
> **Answer**: If the absence record is deleted by the HOS, the teacher reverts to the default PRESENT status and becomes eligible for substitutions during their free periods.

## 8. AVAILABILITY QUESTIONS

### Q8.1: What does FREE mean?
> **Answer**: The faculty member is present on campus and has **no** scheduled class, **no** exam invigilation duty, and **no** accepted substitution commitment during that period.

### Q8.2: What does BUSY mean?
> **Answer**: The faculty member is present on campus, but occupied during that specific period by a class lecture, exam supervision duty, or an accepted substitution.

### Q8.3: What does ABSENT mean?
> **Answer**: The faculty member is not on campus or is marked on leave for that entire calendar date.

### Q8.4: How is availability calculated?
> **Answer**: For a queried date and period, the engine checks four sources:
> 1. Master timetable teaching slots.
> 2. Daily attendance absence records.
> 3. Active exam invigilation duties.
> 4. Accepted substitutions.
> Any match marks the instructor BUSY; otherwise, they are FREE.

### Q8.5: Does teaching make a faculty member BUSY?
> **Answer**: Yes. Having a scheduled lecture or lab in the master timetable makes the teacher BUSY for that period.

### Q8.6: Does exam invigilation make a faculty member BUSY?
> **Answer**: Yes. An assigned exam invigilation duty occupies the teacher and marks them BUSY for that period.

### Q8.7: Does an accepted substitution make a faculty member BUSY?
> **Answer**: Yes. Covering a class for a colleague marks the substitute teacher BUSY for that slot.

### Q8.8: Does an absent faculty member appear as an available candidate?
> **Answer**: No. Teachers marked ABSENT on that date are completely filtered out of the candidate pool.

### Q8.9: How are candidate results ordered?
> **Answer**: Candidates are ordered into two groups:
> 1. **Priority Branch**: Free colleagues from the same department.
> 2. **Other Branches**: Free colleagues from allied departments across the college.

### Q8.10: Why check the same branch first?
> **Answer**: To minimize inter-departmental disruption and utilize local subject expertise before requesting help from other departments.

### Q9.1: How is exam invigilation assigned?
> **Answer**: The HOS can assign an instructor directly to an exam hall for specific periods via `POST /api/invigilation`.

### Q9.2: Can Faculty request exam invigilation?
> **Answer**: Yes. Faculty can submit duty requests via `POST /api/invigilation/requests` specifying desired dates and periods.

### Q9.3: Who approves the invigilation request?
> **Answer**: The Head of Section (HOS) reviews and approves or rejects the request.

### Q9.4: Does invigilation mark the faculty member absent?
> **Answer**: **No.** Invigilation is an active on-campus duty. The teacher is present on campus and only occupied during the assigned examination periods.

### Q9.5: How does invigilation affect availability?
> **Answer**: It marks the teacher BUSY during the assigned exam periods, preventing them from being scheduled for class substitutions.

### Q9.6: Can invigilation conflict with a teaching lecture?
> **Answer**: The system checks for conflicts. An instructor cannot be assigned to invigilate an exam during a period where they already have a scheduled class.

### Q9.7: How are duplicate invigilation assignments prevented?
> **Answer**: The database unique constraint `exam_invigilation_unique` on `(faculty_id, exam_date, period)` prevents duplicate duty assignments.

## 10. MAIN SUBSTITUTION QUESTIONS

### Q10.1: Explain the complete substitution workflow.
> **Answer**:
> 1. Absent Faculty A identifies a vacant teaching slot.
> 2. Faculty A queries the Availability Engine to find genuinely FREE colleagues.
> 3. Faculty A sends a substitution request to Faculty B.
> 4. Faculty B logs in and sees an incoming request card.
> 5. Faculty B clicks Accept (or Reject).
> 6. At acceptance, the server executes real-time atomic revalidation.
> 7. Once confirmed, the substitution is stored in `faculty_substitutions`.

### Q10.2: Who selects the substitute?
> **Answer**: The absent faculty member selects the substitute from the list of free candidates surfaced by the availability engine.

### Q10.3: Can the HOS assign or force a substitution?
> **Answer**: No. The workflow is strictly peer-to-peer (Faculty-to-Faculty).

### Q10.4: Can the HOS approve peer substitutions?
> **Answer**: No. The HOS does not approve or intervene in peer substitutions, eliminating morning bottlenecks. The HOS has read-only visibility in an audit log.

### Q10.5: What happens if Faculty B is already busy?
> **Answer**: Faculty B will be filtered out by the availability engine and cannot be selected.

### Q10.6: What happens if Faculty B becomes busy after the request was sent?
> **Answer**: When Faculty B clicks Accept, the atomic revalidation guard detects the new conflict, aborts acceptance with HTTP 409 Conflict, and notifies Faculty A to pick another colleague.

### Q10.7: How do you prevent double-booking during substitutions?
> **Answer**: Through pre-filtering in the availability engine and atomic real-time revalidation at the exact moment of acceptance.

### Q10.8: Is the master timetable modified during a substitution?
> **Answer**: **No.** The master timetable remains 100% untouched. The substitution is recorded as an overlay in `faculty_substitutions`.

### Q10.9: Where is the substitution stored?
> **Answer**: In the `faculty_substitutions` table, tracking date, period, class, subject, original faculty, substitute faculty, and status.

### Q10.10: Can the same faculty member accept two conflicting substitutions?
> **Answer**: No. As soon as the first substitution is accepted, the teacher becomes BUSY for that slot. Attempting to accept a second request fails revalidation.

### Q10.11: What happens when a request is rejected?
> **Answer**: The status updates to `REJECTED` in `faculty_substitutions`, Faculty A is notified, and Faculty A can select another candidate.

### Q10.12: What happens when a request is accepted?
> **Answer**: The status updates to `ACCEPTED`. The substitute teacher is marked BUSY for that slot, the class is covered, and the event appears in the HOS audit log.

## 11. AI / GEMINI / N8N QUESTIONS

### Q11.1: Why use AI in this project?
> **Answer**: Transcribing scanned paper timetables or PDF matrices into database tables is tedious and prone to human error. AI automates this visual extraction.

### Q11.2: What does Google Gemini do?
> **Answer**: Gemini 2.5 Flash analyzes the visual timetable image/PDF, recognizes days, period numbers, and subjects, and formats the output into strict JSON.

### Q11.3: What does n8n do?
> **Answer**: n8n acts as an automation bridge: it receives upload webhooks, fetches the document binary, calls the Gemini API, and forwards the JSON to our server.

### Q11.4: What input does Gemini receive?
> **Answer**: The timetable file binary (image or PDF) and a strict prompt enforcing the Phase B2.1 JSON contract schema.

### Q11.5: What output does Gemini produce?
> **Answer**: A structured JSON object containing class metadata, days, period timings, subject codes, teacher abbreviations, and multi-period spans.

### Q11.6: What is the Phase B2.1 JSON Contract?
> **Answer**: A strict schema defining the expected JSON structure: required day names, period limits (1–7), session types (`theory`, `lab`, `activity`), and span bounds.

### Q11.7: Why enforce structured JSON?
> **Answer**: Structured JSON allows our backend to programmatically validate, parse, and verify the data before any database interaction.

### Q11.8: What happens if Gemini extracts incorrect or malformed data?
> **Answer**: The backend contract validation gate flags the payload as `INVALID`, logs syntax errors, and blocks approval.

### Q11.9: What happens if an extracted teacher or subject cannot be matched?
> **Answer**: They are marked as `UNRESOLVED`. The system blocks import until the HOS uses the interactive mapping interface to link the text to a registered catalog entity.

### Q11.10: What is timetable staging?
> **Answer**: An intermediate database table (`timetable_staging`) where extracted JSON is held in isolation without touching live class schedules.

### Q11.11: Why is HOS approval required?
> **Answer**: To maintain a human-in-the-loop safety boundary. The department head must verify the extracted schedule before it becomes active.

### Q11.12: Can Gemini directly write to PostgreSQL?
> **Answer**: **No.** Gemini has zero database credentials and cannot execute SQL queries.

### Q11.13: Can n8n directly modify the live timetable?
> **Answer**: **No.** n8n delivers data only to internal staging endpoints protected by `X-Internal-Secret`.

### Q11.14: Why is human approval essential?
> **Answer**: Because LLMs can hallucinate or misread smudged text. Human verification ensures that errors are caught before affecting real classrooms.

## 12. ERROR AND CONFLICT HANDLING

### Q12.1: How are class slot collisions handled?
> **Answer**: Rejection with HTTP 409 Conflict: *"Class already has an entry for this slot"*, enforced by constraint `timetable_class_slot_unique`.

### Q12.2: How is faculty double-booking handled?
> **Answer**: Rejection with HTTP 409 Conflict: *"Faculty is already teaching another class during this period"*, enforced by index `timetable_faculty_slot_unique`.

### Q12.3: How are room conflicts handled?
> **Answer**: Rejection with HTTP 409 Conflict: *"Room is already occupied by another class"*, enforced by index `timetable_room_slot_unique`.

### Q12.4: How are invigilation conflicts handled?
> **Answer**: Rejection with HTTP 409 Conflict if the teacher is already teaching or assigned to another exam hall during that period.

### Q12.5: How are substitution race conditions handled?
> **Answer**: Atomic revalidation at acceptance time aborts the request with HTTP 409 if the substitute was booked by someone else in the interim.

### Q12.6: How are duplicate accounts handled?
> **Answer**: The `users` table enforces `username UNIQUE` and rejects duplicates with HTTP 400 Bad Request.

### Q12.7: How is invalid login handled?
> **Answer**: Returns HTTP 401 Unauthenticated without revealing whether the username or password was incorrect.

### Q12.8: How are inactive faculty handled?
> **Answer**: Inactive accounts are blocked at login and excluded from availability candidate lists.

### Q12.9: How are unresolved AI entities handled?
> **Answer**: The approval endpoint returns HTTP 422 Unprocessable Entity, blocking import until all entities are resolved.

### Q12.10: What if two teachers accept conflicting substitutions simultaneously?
> **Answer**: Database transaction isolation ensures only the first acceptance commits; the second fails revalidation.

## 14. BRANCH ISOLATION QUESTIONS

### Q14.1: Why make branches isolated?
> **Answer**: In polytechnic colleges, each engineering department operates independently. A Computer Engineering head should not have administrative control over Electrical Engineering records.

### Q14.2: How is branch ownership stored?
> **Answer**: Stored in the `departments` table and referenced by foreign keys in `classes`, `faculty`, and `users`.

### Q14.3: How is the HOS restricted to their branch?
> **Answer**: The HOS's branch is encrypted inside their signed session cookie. Backend queries automatically filter by `department_code = req.session.department`.

### Q14.4: How does Faculty inherit their branch?
> **Answer**: When created by an HOS, the faculty record automatically inherits the HOS's branch code and cannot change it.

### Q14.5: Can an HOS modify another branch?
> **Answer**: No. Cross-branch write requests are intercepted and rejected with HTTP 403 Forbidden.

### Q14.6: How does cross-branch availability work?
> **Answer**: The availability engine searches the entire college for free teachers, but displays local department candidates first under 'Priority Branch', followed by other departments under 'Other Branches'.

### Q14.7: Why allow cross-branch availability while keeping management isolated?
> **Answer**: Because when all local department teachers are busy, teaching capacity in allied departments can cover classes, maximizing college-wide teacher utilization without compromising administrative security.

## 15. SECURITY SCENARIO QUESTIONS

### Q15.1: What if a Faculty member tries to access an HOS route?
> **Answer**: Express middleware `requireHOS` detects `role === 'faculty'` and immediately responds with HTTP 403 Forbidden.

### Q15.2: What if an inactive Faculty member tries to login?
> **Answer**: The login handler checks `active === TRUE`. Inactive accounts are rejected with an account disabled error.

### Q15.3: What if two Faculty members request the same substitute simultaneously?
> **Answer**: Both can send a request, but whichever substitution is accepted first commits. The second acceptance will fail real-time revalidation.

### Q15.4: What if a substitute teacher becomes busy before clicking Accept?
> **Answer**: The atomic revalidation guard detects the conflict at the moment of clicking Accept and aborts with HTTP 409 Conflict.

### Q15.5: What if Gemini extracts an incorrect faculty name?
> **Answer**: It will not match any registered catalog entry and will be flagged as `UNRESOLVED`, blocking import until the HOS manually maps or corrects it.

### Q15.6: What if an uploaded timetable contains a duplicate slot?
> **Answer**: The Phase B2.3 contract validator catches the duplicate day/period slot in JSON, marks the upload `INVALID`, and logs the error.

## 16. TRICK QUESTIONS (AVOIDING EXAGGERATED CLAIMS)

### Q16.1: Does AI automatically choose and assign the best substitute?
> **Answer**: **No.** The system never automatically assigns substitutes. The absent teacher selects a colleague, and the colleague must explicitly accept.

### Q16.2: Does the HOS have to approve every faculty substitution?
> **Answer**: **No.** Substitutions are peer-to-peer (Faculty-to-Faculty) to avoid morning administrative delays. The HOS only has read-only audit visibility.

### Q16.3: Does substitution alter the master timetable?

## 18. TOP 10 QUESTIONS TO MEMORIZE FOR THE VIVA

1. **Explain the peer-to-peer substitution workflow.**  
   *Absent Faculty A selects a free colleague (Faculty B) from the availability engine and sends a request. Faculty B reviews and accepts it. The server executes real-time atomic revalidation to ensure Faculty B is still free, commits the substitution to an overlay table, and leaves the master timetable untouched.*

2. **Why does the HOS not approve peer substitutions?**  
   *In technical colleges, morning absences require immediate coverage before classes begin. Requiring HOS approval creates an administrative bottleneck. Peer-to-peer coordination allows coverage in two minutes, while the HOS retains full visibility via read-only audit logs.*

3. **What is the difference between ABSENT, BUSY, and FREE?**  
   *ABSENT means the teacher is on leave for the entire day. BUSY means the teacher is in college but occupied during that period by a class, exam duty, or accepted substitution. FREE means the teacher is in college and has no conflicting duties for that period.*

4. **How do you guarantee zero double-booking?**  
   *We enforce it at two levels: in-memory availability screening before requests are processed, and PostgreSQL partial unique constraints (`timetable_faculty_slot_unique`, `timetable_room_slot_unique`) at the database engine level.*

5. **Why use PostgreSQL on Neon instead of MongoDB?**  
   *Academic timetables are inherently relational with strict dependencies between departments, classes, subjects, rooms, and periods. PostgreSQL enforces foreign keys, ACID transactions, and unique indexes to prevent invalid bookings.*

6. **Why can't Gemini AI write directly to the database?**  
   *AI models can hallucinate or misread abbreviated names. Allowing direct database access could silently corrupt production timetables. Isolating output in a staging table allows the HOS to review and approve data safely.*

7. **How does multi-tenant branch isolation work?**  
   *The user's department is encrypted inside their signed session cookie. All backend SQL queries are parameterized by that department code. A Computer Engineering HOS cannot view or edit Electrical Engineering records.*

8. **What happens during exam invigilation?**  
   *The instructor is assigned to an exam hall for specific periods. The availability engine marks them BUSY for those periods, preventing substitution clashes while recognizing that they are present on campus.*

9. **What happens if a substitute teacher becomes busy before accepting?**  
   *When the teacher clicks Accept, our backend runs an atomic real-time revalidation query. If a conflict occurred in the interim, acceptance is aborted with HTTP 409 Conflict, and the requester is notified.*

10. **What are your verified testing results?**  
    *30/30 database checks passed, 10 core entities persisted across server restart, all 29 automated test suites passed, and 48/48 live real-world verification checks passed.*
