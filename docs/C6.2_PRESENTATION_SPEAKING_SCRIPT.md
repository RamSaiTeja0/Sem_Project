## 1. 30-SECOND INTRODUCTION

> *"Good morning, respected evaluators. My project is **TecSubstitution**, a centralized academic timetable and faculty substitution management system. In polytechnic and engineering colleges, sudden teacher absences cause vacant lecture hours, and manual substitution leads to double-booking and confusion. Our system digitally manages class timetables, tracks daily teacher attendance, calculates real-time teacher availability, and provides a direct, conflict-free peer-to-peer substitution workflow backed by a secure PostgreSQL cloud database."*

## 2. 1-MINUTE PROJECT EXPLANATION

> *"In our college, weekly timetables are tightly packed across multiple semesters and laboratories. When a faculty member takes sudden leave or goes for examination invigilation duty, finding a free teacher is stressful and time-consuming. Phone calls and paper registers often result in double-booking a teacher who already has a lecture.*
> 
> *TecSubstitution solves this problem completely. The Head of Section (HOS) manages the department timetable, classes, and daily attendance. When a teacher is absent, our availability engine instantly checks the master schedule, exam duties, and attendance to find who is genuinely free.*
> 
> *The absent teacher can directly send a substitution request to an available colleague. The colleague accepts or declines in their own portal, with automatic real-time conflict checking. The master timetable remains completely safe and untouched, and the HOS gets full visibility through an audit log."*

## 3. 2-MINUTE PRESENTATION SCRIPT

> *"Respected evaluators, today I present **TecSubstitution**, built to modernize academic scheduling and eliminate substitution clashes in technical institutions.*
> 
> *Our application is built with a Node.js and Express backend, a clean responsive Vanilla JavaScript and CSS frontend, and a remote serverless PostgreSQL database on Neon. We chose a relational database because academic timetables require strict integrity: foreign keys, primary keys, and unique indexes to guarantee zero double-booking.*
> 
> *The system operates on two core roles: Head of Section and Faculty Member. Each HOS manages their own branch—such as Computer Engineering or Electrical Engineering—with strict departmental isolation. One department cannot modify or view another department's protected records.*
> 
> *When a teacher takes leave, the HOS records their absence for that specific date. Our Availability Engine instantly updates: it marks the absent teacher as busy, screens out teachers who have classes or exam supervision duties, and displays available candidates, prioritizing teachers from the same department first.*
> 
> *The core innovation is our **peer-to-peer substitution workflow**. An absent teacher selects a free colleague and dispatches a request. The colleague reviews the request and clicks Accept. At that exact moment, our server revalidates availability to ensure no conflict occurred in between. Once accepted, the covering teacher is marked busy, while the original master timetable remains untouched.*
> 
> *We also integrated an AI extraction pipeline: scanned timetable images or PDFs are parsed by Google Gemini Vision through an n8n workflow. Crucially, the AI writes only to a staging table. The HOS must inspect, resolve any unmatched names, and approve the timetable before it enters production.*
> 
> *The entire system has been verified through 29 automated test suites, 30 database constraint checks, persistence testing across server restarts, and 48 live end-to-end operational checks."*

## 5. MAIN FEATURE EXPLANATION (PEER-TO-PEER SUBSTITUTION)

> *"Our main feature is the **Faculty-to-Faculty Substitution Workflow** (Option A).
> 
> When Faculty A is absent:
> 1. Faculty A views their vacant teaching period.
> 2. The system surfaces genuinely FREE colleagues.
> 3. Faculty A sends a substitution request to Faculty B.
> 4. Faculty B logs in, reviews the details, and clicks Accept or Reject.
> 5. If accepted, the server immediately revalidates Faculty B's availability to prevent race conditions or double-booking.
> 6. The substitution is confirmed and recorded.
> 
> **Important Rule**: The HOS does NOT approve or assign peer substitutions. This is strictly faculty-to-faculty so classes can be covered in minutes. The HOS has read-only visibility through an audit ledger, and the master timetable is never overwritten."*

## 6. AI + n8n EXPLANATION

> *"For timetable digitization, we implemented an AI extraction pipeline:
> 
> **Image or PDF $\longrightarrow$ n8n Webhook $\longrightarrow$ Google Gemini 2.5 Flash $\longrightarrow$ Strict B2.1 JSON $\longrightarrow$ Backend Validation $\longrightarrow$ Staging Table $\longrightarrow$ HOS Approval $\longrightarrow$ Live Timetable.**
> 
> **Why doesn't the AI write directly to PostgreSQL?**
> Because AI models can hallucinate or misread smudged teacher abbreviations. If an AI wrote directly to the database, it could silently corrupt class schedules or overwrite existing faculty records. By isolating AI output in a staging table, we enforce a strict human-in-the-loop safety boundary: the HOS must review the extracted schedule, map any unrecognized teacher names, and approve the import before anything touches the live timetable."*

---

## 7. DATABASE EXPLANATION ("Why PostgreSQL / Neon?")

> *"We chose **PostgreSQL on Neon** for five clear technical reasons:
> 1. **Relational Structure**: Timetables are inherently relational—departments employ faculty, faculty teach subjects, subjects belong to classes, and classes sit in rooms during periods.
> 2. **Database-Level Constraints**: PostgreSQL enforces unique indexes (`timetable_faculty_slot_unique`, `timetable_room_slot_unique`) so double-booking is physically rejected by the database engine.
> 3. **ACID Transactions**: When importing an approved timetable or accepting a substitution, atomic transactions ensure all-or-nothing data consistency.
> 4. **Durability & Persistence**: Unlike in-memory data, PostgreSQL guarantees that schedules, attendance records, and substitutions survive server restarts and power loss.
> 5. **Serverless Cloud Hosting**: Neon provides managed, SSL-encrypted cloud PostgreSQL with connection pooling, making the system accessible from anywhere."*

---

## 8. SECURITY EXPLANATION

> *"TecSubstitution implements multi-layered security:
> 1. **Stateless Signed Sessions**: When a user logs in, the server issues a `tec_session` cookie signed with HMAC-SHA256. If anyone tampers with the cookie, signature verification fails immediately.
> 2. **Role-Based Guards**: Express middleware strictly verifies roles (`requireHOS`, `requireFaculty`). Faculty cannot access HOS settings, and HOS cannot forge peer substitutions.
> 3. **Departmental Branch Isolation**: The user's department is locked into their session. Every query is filtered by department. A CME HOS cannot view or edit EEE records.
> 4. **Strict Password Complexity**: The system enforces a minimum of 6 characters containing uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and an underscore.
> 5. **SQL Injection Defense**: All database queries use parameterized statements (`$1, $2`) via the `pg` driver, completely preventing SQL injection."*

## 9. AVAILABILITY EXPLANATION (ABSENT vs BUSY vs FREE)

> *"In our system, teacher availability is defined with mathematical precision:
> 
> - **ABSENT**: The faculty member is on leave or marked absent for that calendar date. They cannot be approached for any substitution that day.
> - **BUSY**: The faculty member is present on campus, but they cannot take a substitution during that specific period because they are teaching a regular class, supervising an exam, or already covering an accepted substitution.
> - **FREE**: The faculty member is present on campus, has no class scheduled in the master timetable, has no exam invigilation duty, and has no other substitution commitments during that period. Only FREE teachers can be requested for substitution."*

## 13. COMMON FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS & CRISP ANSWERS

### Q1: Why PostgreSQL?
> *"Because timetables are relational. Classes, faculty, subjects, rooms, and periods have foreign key dependencies, and PostgreSQL allows us to enforce unique constraints at the database level to prevent double-booking."*

### Q2: Why Neon?
> *"Neon provides serverless cloud PostgreSQL with connection pooling and SSL encryption, meaning our database is accessible over the internet without needing a local database server running on our PC."*

### Q3: Why n8n?
> *"n8n is an open-source visual workflow engine. It allows us to handle file webhooks, communicate with external AI APIs, and manage retries asynchronously without slowing down our main Node.js web server."*

### Q4: Why Gemini?
> *"Google Gemini 2.5 Flash has multimodal vision capabilities, allowing it to accurately read complex visual grid structures, periods, and subject abbreviations from scanned JPEG and PDF timetable sheets."*

### Q5: Why not let AI directly modify the database?
> *"Because AI models can hallucinate or misread names. Directly modifying production data could overwrite real faculty records. Keeping AI output in a staging table allows the HOS to review and approve data safely."*

### Q6: Why Node.js and Express?
> *"Node.js provides fast, asynchronous, non-blocking I/O ideal for handling multiple concurrent teacher requests, and Express provides lightweight, flexible routing without framework bloat."*

### Q7: How do you prevent double-booking?
> *"At two levels: first, our backend availability engine validates slots before writing; second, PostgreSQL partial unique indexes (`timetable_faculty_slot_unique`, `timetable_room_slot_unique`) reject duplicate bookings at the SQL level."*

### Q8: How does branch isolation work?
> *"The user's department is encrypted inside their signed session cookie. Every database query automatically filters by that department code. A Computer Engineering HOS cannot query or edit Electrical Engineering records."*

### Q9: How does the availability engine work?
> *"It checks a target date and period against four things: master timetable teaching slots, daily attendance absence records, exam invigilation duties, and accepted substitutions. Any conflict marks the teacher BUSY."*

### Q10: What happens when a faculty member is absent?
> *"The HOS marks them ABSENT for that specific date in the attendance module. The availability engine immediately excludes that teacher from being suggested as a substitute for any period that day."*

### Q11: Who selects the substitute?
> *"The absent faculty member selects the substitute from the list of genuinely free colleagues surfaced by the availability engine."*

### Q12: Can the HOS approve or force a substitution?
> *"No. The substitution workflow is strictly peer-to-peer (Faculty-to-Faculty) so arrangements can happen quickly without administrative bottlenecks. The HOS has read-only visibility in an audit log."*

### Q13: What happens if the selected faculty becomes busy before accepting?
> *"Our backend performs atomic real-time revalidation at the exact moment the colleague clicks Accept. If they became busy in the interim, the acceptance is aborted with HTTP 409 Conflict."*

### Q14: What is the difference between ABSENT and BUSY?
> *"ABSENT means the teacher is not in college for the whole day. BUSY means the teacher is in college, but occupied during that specific period by a class, exam duty, or another substitution."*

### Q15: What happens during exam invigilation?
> *"When assigned to invigilate an exam, the teacher is marked BUSY for those specific periods in the availability engine, preventing teaching substitution clashes while recognizing they are on campus."*

### Q16: Does invigilation mean the faculty is absent?
> *"No. Invigilation is an active institutional duty. The teacher is present on campus and only occupied during the examination periods."*

### Q17: What happens if the AI extracts incorrect data?
> *"The extraction is validated against our Phase B2.3 contract schema. If invalid, it is flagged as INVALID and approval is blocked. If valid but names are mismatched, the HOS maps them in the review panel."*

### Q18: What happens when timetable entities cannot be matched?
> *"They are marked as UNRESOLVED. The system blocks approval until the HOS uses the interactive mapping interface to map the extracted text to an existing faculty member or subject."*

### Q19: Is the master timetable changed when a substitution is accepted?
> *"No. The original master timetable is 100% untouched. Substitutions are stored in a separate `faculty_substitutions` table as an overlay, preserving semester schedules for future weeks."*

### Q20: What are the main limitations?
> *"The system requires an active internet connection for cloud PostgreSQL and Gemini, handles 1-to-1 period substitutions rather than splitting periods, and relies on session cookies rather than enterprise SSO."*

### Q21: What is the future scope?
> *"Adding SMS and WhatsApp push notifications, smart AI recommendation scoring based on workload, visual departmental analytics, automated timetable generation from scratch, and native mobile apps."*
